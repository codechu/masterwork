# Growing a corpus

The starter ([`corpus/starter/tales.md`](starter/tales.md) and [`script.json`](starter/script.json)) is three tale-and-question pairs. It is not a small version of a
working corpus; it is the shape of one. What follows is what a house needs to
know to grow its own, which is the only kind that will fit it.

## The invariant

**One tale, one question.** Every tale in the corpus has exactly one question
in the script, and every question has exactly one tale. Add a tale and you
add a question in the same change, or the ceremony silently stops measuring
whatever the new tale teaches.

## Why tales and not instructions

A tale gives a pattern; an instruction gives a rule. Rules are followed where
they are recognised and nowhere else, and a rule about the situation you
described does not reach the situation you did not.

Two failures worth knowing before you spend a month on them:

- **Assigned traits do not stick.** "Behave like a careful engineer" is worn
  while it is in view and dropped in the next fresh context. What survives is
  a pattern the model re-derived and committed to in its own words.
- **Compressed tales die.** A tale cut down to a single summarising sentence
  stops working — measurably, not aesthetically. What it becomes is an
  aphorism: no scene, no cost, nothing that fires in a situation it never
  described. If a pattern is worth keeping, it is worth its scene.

Write in the third person, about a craft, with a concrete cost. Not about
agents, tools or models: a pattern told through a cooper's barrel reaches a
situation nobody described, while a story about tool calls reaches tool calls.

**One focus per tale.** A tale that teaches three things teaches none of them
well enough to survive the ceremony.

## Where a tale comes from

Not from imagination, and not from a list of virtues. A tale comes from
something the house measured and got wrong. That is what makes the corpus
worth having and what makes it yours: it is a record of your failures in a
form a model can inherit.

The order that works is: the failure happens; it is measured; the pattern
behind it is named; a tale is written that has that pattern as its single
focus; a question is written that asks for the commitment without naming the
tale.

## Stitching

Tales should live in each other's scenes. A motif that appears once, in one
tale, stays inert. A motif touched by several tales in passing — a scale, a
slate, a name written on something — comes through into behaviour.

Stitch backwards only. A new tale reaches back to scenes that already exist;
it does not leave a thread for a tale that has not been written, and it does
not invent a detail in an old tale to attach to. If there is no existing
half-thread, do not force one.

## Questions

The question is the expensive half. It must:

- ask for a commitment, not a description — "what do you do" rather than
  "what is important";
- never name its tale, or the model answers the tale instead of the axis;
- carry several clauses, because a single-clause question gets a single-clause
  answer that reads well and commits to nothing;
- ask what it costs when the commitment is not kept.

Expect to rewrite a question two or three times, watching what comes back
each time. A question that produces confident, agreeable text which commits
to nothing is the common failure and it is easy to miss.

## Layer order

Tales, then the commitment the model writes, then its name, and instructions
**last** if there are any at all. An instruction belongs in a corpus only
when a pattern needs a concrete shape a story cannot carry — an exact format,
a syntax. It goes at the end, outside the ceremony, and it gives form only:
never a reason, never a method. Those are the tale's work.

If the instruction section is growing, the corpus is drifting into a rulebook.

## Tales that cannot be measured

A tale whose effect no measurement can separate is not a failed tale, and
deleting it throws away what it cost to find. Tag it instead — mark it
unmeasured, leave the text intact, and have the ceremony skip it along with
its question, so the one-to-one invariant survives. The tag comes off when a
measurement shows something, and the measurement gets written down next to it.

## The trap at the end

The corpus is not the goal. A character is worth having because an agent that
marks what it has actually seen, refuses a premise it cannot find, and prices
the wall it has hit does better work. If a tale cannot be traced to work that
got better, it is decoration, however well it reads.
